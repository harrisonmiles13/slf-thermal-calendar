How to read it

Insects develop when it is warm enough, so their seasons track accumulated heat better than the calendar. Each map cell adds up the daily heat above 10 °C (50 °F) from January 1 and records the day it passes a threshold.

  • Date reached: the day the threshold was passed in the chosen year. Hatched cells have not reached it yet.
  • vs. normal: days earlier (red) or later (blue) than the 1991–2020 average.
  • Normal and Variability: the 30-year mean date and its standard deviation.
  • Years reached: how often a stage is reached at all. Where egg laying rarely completes, the climate limits SLF.
  • Stage on date: the most advanced SLF stage on any day; press Play to watch the season move north.

Click anywhere for the stage dates and heat curve at that spot. In the current year the panel adds live weather and a 16-day forecast from Open-Meteo, and projects later stages assuming normal temperatures. Points outside the contiguous US (for example southern Canada) use Open-Meteo for both the season and the normal.

The SLF model

Degree-days use the DDRP spotted lanternfly model of Barker, Beyer & Coop (2025): lower threshold 10 °C, upper threshold 35 °C with a horizontal cutoff, single-triangle method, started on January 1.

Data and limits

  • Temperature: NOAA nClimGrid-Daily (contiguous US, about 5 km), averaged to 1/12° (about 8 km). Recent weeks use NOAA's preliminary grids, which are revised later. Maps refresh daily.
  • SLF records: survey data aggregated to 10 km cells by lydemapr v4.1.0 (De Bona et al. 2023), through 2025. Filled dots mark cells with an established population, open dots cells with any other SLF record. Absence of a dot does not mean SLF is absent.
  • Grid cells average terrain and microclimate; sunny walls, cities and valley floors can run days ahead of the map.
  • Model output is a planning aid for scouting and treatment timing. Confirm stages in the field.
